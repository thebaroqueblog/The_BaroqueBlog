The Book

Die Dynastie der Geigenbaufamilie Ficker

Town church of Markneukirchen

The question

Many instruments by the Fickers bear neither a label nor a brand stamp. Can they nevertheless be attributed to a particular maker within the family? This is the question the book sets out to answer.

It also tells the story of the family, their workshops and their instruments in Markneukirchen.

Markneukirchen

The violin makers who settled in Markneukirchen in the 17th century came as Protestant exiles from Bohemia. In their homeland they were required to convert to the Catholic faith. Remaining true to their beliefs, they took their tools and their knowledge with them and fled across the border into the Vogtland.

For generations, the Tyrolean violin maker Jakob Stainer (c. 1617–1683) served as their model. On 6 March 1677 the founding of the violin makers’ guild of Markneukirchen was officially confirmed. The charter names twelve masters who had come from Graslitz.

According to Bernhard Zöbisch, Vogtländischer Geigenbau bis 1850.

A mystery at the beginning

At the beginning of the family stands an enigmatic figure: Johann Christian Ficker I. Little is known about him. A large part of Markneukirchen’s written records, including numerous church registers, was lost in a town fire in 1840. He does not appear as a master of the violin makers’ guild.

His instrument labels identify him as a violin maker around 1700. Whether he was the ancestor of the family has not been proven.

According to Bernhard Zöbisch, Vogtländischer Geigenbau bis 1850.

How I work

The basis is instruments whose origin is secure: through a label, a brand stamp or a documented provenance. I collect photographs and measurements, compare the instruments with one another and evaluate historical sources.

Where an attribution is only probable and not proven, I say so.

Structure of the book

  1. The cradle of Vogtland violin making
  2. The economic and social network of the violin-making dynasties
  3. Johann Christian Ficker I
  4. The Caspar line (Line I)
  5. The Heinrich line (Line II)
